NASA SPACE APPS 2026 · CHALLENGE: EARTH JUKEBOX

NASA Earth Information Center Sonification, Data Provenance & Disclosures

Built for the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — "Earth Jukebox" category. The application pairs EOSDIS NASA GIBS imagery layers matching key themes of the NASA Earth Information Center (EIC) (Sea Ice / Cryosphere, Surface Temperature, Active Thermal Anomalies & Fire, Earth at Night Lights, True Color Land/Atmosphere, and Ocean Chlorophyll Biosphere) with deterministic Web Audio sonification and tactile accessibility. When a live GIBS frame fails to load, sonification automatically mutes so placeholder pixels are never presented as Earth data.

NASA & THIRD-PARTY DATA SOURCES USED
NASA EOSDIS GIBS (WMTS & WMS)

MODIS Terra TrueColor, VIIRS Day/Night Band (SNPP & NOAA-20), MODIS Thermal Anomalies, Sea Ice Concentration (MODIS Terra / AMSR-E / AMSR2 / GHRSST MUR), MODIS Monthly Land Surface Temp, and Ocean Chlorophyll (SeaWiFS / MODIS Aqua / VIIRS NOAA-20).

NASA POWER API (LaRC)

Langley Research Center multi-decadal climatology baselines (T2M, RH2M, ALLSKY_SFC_SW_DWN, WS10M, PS) used for anomaly comparison in regional telemetry.

NASA EONET v3 API (GSFC)

Earth Observatory Natural Event Tracker live hazard pins for wildfires, storms, and volcanic events.

Open-Meteo Weather & Copernicus CAMS

Open-Meteo Forecast API (local meteorology) and Open-Meteo Air Quality API powered by Copernicus CAMS (PM2.5, PM10, CO, NO₂, O₃, SO₂, UV, AOD). Reverse geocoding by OpenStreetMap Nominatim; ISS coordinates via WhereTheISS.at.

EARTH JUKEBOX FRAME-TO-SOUND MAPPINGS
Visual Feature Audio Parameter Reason for the Choice
Average Hue (0–360°) Melody pitch quantized to a C minor pentatonic scale Quantizing hue to a consonant minor pentatonic scale prevents jarring microtonal dissonance while letting ocean blues, vegetated greens, and warm thermal hues stay clearly distinguishable.
Mean Brightness Master output level & lowpass filter cutoff (260–4200 Hz) Bright sunlit regions open the spectral filter so the sound feels airy and luminous, whereas dark night or polar winter scenes sound softer and warmer.
Ice / Cloud Fraction Low steady polar drone vs. high shimmering harmonic Thick bright ice or cloud cover reinforces a grounding low drone (low tone = more ice/ocean), while melting ice or warm open surface lifts high shimmering overtones.
Fire / Heat Fraction Rhythm density & detuned oscillator dissonance Introducing beating detune and faster rhythmic pulses signals active thermal anomalies or extreme heat immediately without relying on harsh white noise.
Edge Density Pulse / tempo rate (0.8–6.0 Hz) Sharp coastlines, fragmented ice floes, and fire fronts carry high spatial gradients, naturally translating into brisker rhythmic pulses that can also drive tactile haptics.
Live Telemetry (Temp, Precip, PM2.5) Subtle secondary filter warmth, brightness & haze nudge Keeps the visible satellite frame as the primary driver while letting real-time local atmospheric telemetry gently color the timbre.

Why we chose these mappings

Many data sonifications map raw numbers directly to unquantized sine sweeps or noisy static, which quickly causes listener fatigue. Instead, we anchored hue to a gentle minor pentatonic scale over a soft harmonic pad, pairing intuitive physical metaphors—low steady drones for ice and ocean mass, bright open filters for sunlight, brisk pulses for fractured edges, and detuned harmonic tension for wildfire heat—so blind, low-vision, and sighted listeners alike can perceive planetary change musically, deterministically, and repeatably from 2000 through 2026.

Use of AI & Procedural Synthesis Disclosure

Ambient soundscapes and frame tones are generated procedurally using the browser Web Audio API (oscillators and filtered noise, not pre-recorded spacecraft or field audio). Pixel analysis and multi-year preset trend metrics use deterministic JavaScript math (0 random values) over live NASA GIBS imagery. AI coding assistance helped refactor JS structure, ARIA semantics, and documentation; all data pipelines and audio formulas are human-reviewed.

Accessibility & Validation Scope

Automated browser tests verify keyboard navigation, aria-live descriptions, aria-valuetext time slider updates, offline muting, multi-year GIBS calculations, WAV export, and raised-dot 3D Braille STL/OBJ meshes. Live co-testing with blind and low-vision listeners remains the next validation milestone (no synthetic user study claimed). See README.md in the project repository for setup and submission details.
