EARTH JUKEBOX FRAME-TO-SOUND MAPPINGS
Visual Feature Audio Parameter Reason for the Choice
Average Hue (0–360°) Melody pitch quantized to a C minor pentatonic scale Quantizing hue to a consonant minor pentatonic scale prevents jarring microtonal dissonance while letting ocean blues, vegetated greens, and warm thermal hues stay clearly distinguishable.
Mean Brightness Master output level & lowpass filter cutoff (260–4200 Hz) Bright sunlit regions open the spectral filter so the sound feels airy and luminous, whereas dark night or polar winter scenes sound softer and warmer.
Ice / Cloud Fraction Low steady polar drone vs. high shimmering harmonic Thick bright ice or cloud cover reinforces a grounding low drone (low tone = more ice/ocean), while melting ice or warm open surface lifts high shimmering overtones.
Fire / Heat Fraction Rhythm density & detuned oscillator dissonance Introducing beating detune and faster rhythmic pulses signals active thermal anomalies or extreme heat immediately without relying on harsh white noise.
Edge Density Pulse / tempo rate (0.8–6.0 Hz) Sharp coastlines, fragmented ice floes, and fire fronts carry high spatial gradients, naturally translating into brisker rhythmic pulses that can also drive tactile haptics.
Live Telemetry (Temp, Precip, PM2.5) Subtle secondary filter warmth, brightness & haze nudge Keeps the visible satellite frame as the primary driver while letting real-time local atmospheric telemetry gently color the timbre.

Why we chose these mappings

Many data sonifications map raw numbers directly to unquantized sine sweeps or noisy static, which quickly causes listener fatigue. Instead, we anchored hue to a gentle minor pentatonic scale over a soft harmonic pad, pairing intuitive physical metaphors—low steady drones for ice and ocean mass, bright open filters for sunlight, brisk pulses for fractured edges, and detuned harmonic tension for wildfire heat—so blind, low-vision, and sighted listeners alike can perceive planetary change musically, deterministically, and repeatably from 2000 through 2026.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS & SCIENTIFIC SCOPE
  • Projection approximation: cos(latitude) row weighting approximates equal-area Area in EPSG:4326 tiles; high Arctic polar stereographic projection distortion above ~85°N is truncated cleanly at row weights ≥ 0.05.
  • Sensor continuity & coastlines: Sea ice uses GHRSST L4 MUR (Jun 2002–Oct 2026) and MODIS Terra before Jun 2002; Ocean color transitions SeaWiFS → MODIS Aqua → VIIRS NOAA-20. Transitions are announced explicitly per frame.
  • Swath gaps & low coverage: When valid non-fill data covers under 40% of the frame, output level scales down and a clear low-coverage notice is shown.
  • User study status: External blind/low-vision usability evaluation protocol & self-check tool are shipped; live participant study status reads dynamically from local storage (currently not yet run unless recorded on this browser).

Earth Jukebox · Classroom Worksheet (Satellite Sonification)

Built with NASA open data (GIBS + POWER + NSIDC reference) · Aligned with Earth Science & Accessibility learning objectives

  1. Arctic Sea Ice Time-Travel (Sept 2003 vs Sept 2026): Listen to the Arctic Sea Ice track in 2003 and then in 2026 (or turn on A/B Stereo Compare). How do pitch height and ocean-vs-ice balance change as sea ice retreats?
    Answer key: Thick white sea ice in 2003 sustains a heavier low polar drone and lower average brightness-weighted shimmer; reduced ice concentration in 2026 exposes dark open ocean, dropping the low ice drone weight and raising pelagic hue pitch / shimmer.
  2. Why Quantize Hue to a Musical Scale? Explain why mapping satellite hue directly to continuous unquantized frequencies creates listener fatigue, and describe how Earth Jukebox's scale library solves this.
    Answer key: Continuous frequency glissandos clash and hide gradual trends; snapping hue degrees to discrete scale intervals (such as C minor pentatonic or Bhairavi) keeps transitions musical while preserving monotonic pitch direction.
  3. Multi-Sensory Scientific Honesty: Why does Earth Jukebox mute audio output when NASA GIBS tiles are offline or below 40% valid-data coverage?
    Answer key: Sonifying placeholder graphics or nearly empty swaths would mislead listeners into mistaking synthetic colors or missing satellite passes for real Earth measurements.
